Medical SDEC Demand and Capacity Tool

A bottom-up model of Same Day Emergency Care demand and capacity at Lister, built area by area. Each area — the two bays, the ambulatory chairs, the hot clinic rooms, the darting rooms and triage — carries its own spaces, its own staff and its own hourly demand; the floor-wide numbers are the roll-up. Set the current state, then test a future state by adjusting demand and capacity assumptions. Every input is editable.

Method

How to Use the Model

STEP 1
Capacity Inputs
Give each area its physical spaces and its own roster — WTE, shift start and end, and the expected patients seen per person per hour. Staff who circulate go in the floating pool.
STEP 2
Demand Inputs
Enter the average patients present in each area each hour, 07:00–24:00. One row per area; the floor profile is their sum. Switch between weekday and weekend.
STEP 3
Current State
Review demand against capacity hour by hour — medical, nursing and physical space — for the whole floor and then area by area, to see which area is actually the problem.
STEP 4
Future State
Add a nurse, a doctor or physical space to a named area, dial demand up or down, and read the change against today — including an indicative cost or saving.
Step 1

Capacity Inputs — Per Area

SDEC is not one pool: each area has its own physical spaces, its own staff and its own demand. Give each area below its trolleys, chairs and rooms, then its roster — WTE, shift start and end times, and the expected patients seen per person per hour for each medical grade. Each line also has its own Cost/WTE, defaulting to the global assumption but overridable where actual grade/band costs are known. For nursing, tick “Holds patients?” on the lines that hold patients in that area — these add holding capacity at the patients-per-nurse figure on the line, and are then capped at the area’s physical spaces. Staff who circulate rather than sitting in one area go in the Floating / unassigned pool at the bottom; they count towards floor-wide capacity and are attributed to areas each hour in proportion to that area’s demand. Move any line between areas with its Area dropdown. The per-area split of staffing is a mechanical placeholder from the old floor-wide roster — confirm the real establishment area by area.

Bay 1, Bay 2, Darting and Triage carry no placeholder demand at all, because nothing in the referral-source split maps to them; nothing was invented. Replace these rows with real per-area attendance as soon as the SDEC team can supply it. Arrivals per hour are derived from each area’s profile and the assumed length of stay.

Step 3

Current State — Demand vs Capacity

Today’s demand against today’s capacity, hour by hour, for the selected day type — first for the whole floor, then area by area. Three questions are asked separately and never added together. Medical: can the clinicians on shift keep pace with arrivals, or does a queue build? Nursing: are there enough nurses for the patients present? Space: are there enough trolleys, chairs and rooms to put them in? A floor-wide “within capacity” reading can hide one area quietly overflowing — the “By area” view below is where you find out.

What This Means
    Demand vs Capacity, by Hour
    Pick a view below. Overall service shows the floor-wide hour-by-hour status — within capacity, or which of medical, nursing and physical space is exceeded — because a flow measure, a concurrency measure and a hard ceiling are never added together. By area repeats that classification for every area against its own demand, roster and spaces. Medical detail shows arrivals against seeing capacity and the queue that forms. Holding detail shows patients present against nursing capacity and physical spaces. Each detail chart has its own hour-by-hour balance bar underneath: blue/green means spare capacity, red means demand is exceeding capacity that hour.
    Step 4

    Future State — Demand vs Capacity

    Test a change. On the demand side, dial each referral source up or down (or type an explicit attendances/day figure), apply an overall growth factor, or model better flow with a shorter length of stay — a per-source dial reaches each area through that area’s own referral mix. On the capacity side, switch on a ready-made preset lever, add physical spaces to a named area, or pull in every current-state shift line and add to it directly; every addition targets a specific area, or the floating pool. The charts below redraw for the future state, with the current state shown as a faint dashed line for comparison, and the headline change — including the net WTE and cost of every addition — quantified below.

      Data to Confirm

      What the SDEC Team Needs to Supply or Confirm to Firm Up This Template:
      Real per-area staffing — headcounts and shift patterns area by area. The old roster was not area-tagged at all, so the bay nursing lines have simply been split evenly across Bay 1 and Bay 2 and the EMT lines put in Darting
      Real per-area hourly attendance — the area × hour grid in Step 2 is currently a mechanical split of the floor-wide profile by referral source; Bay 1, Bay 2, Darting and Triage have no demand at all
      Whether the 40 general chairs have any dedicated staff or are covered by floating/bay nurses — they currently have no nursing establishment of their own, so only the 40-space ceiling constrains them
      Whether “patients present” already includes time in the Hot Clinic, Darting and Triage rooms, or whether those are shorter, separate throughput events with different concurrency dynamics from a bay stay
      Whether the one usable Triage room holds patients (an occupancy to model) or is a pure process step with no “present” time at all
      Which clinicians are genuinely fixed to an area — every medical line is currently floating, on the assumption that clinicians circulate; a GP who only ever runs hot clinics should be re-tagged
      Nursing shift start/end times — the template gives roles and counts but not timings (roster lead / Claudia)
      SHO patients-per-hour rate — assumed 0.5/hr within the 0.5–1 envelope; confirm from job plans
      Mean and distribution of length of stay by area and by referral source, to replace the single ~5h data-implied value applied everywhere
      Patients-per-nurse ratio the bays are actually run to (assumed ~1:4)
      Hot-clinic schedule by day of week (frailty, cardiology, rheumatology, ACT/respiratory) — demand is day-specific and booked to a template
      Whether weekend capacity lines and demand should be modelled separately per Saturday and Sunday
      Arrivals-by-hour per area (not just presence-by-hour) if available, to replace the derived arrivals curve
