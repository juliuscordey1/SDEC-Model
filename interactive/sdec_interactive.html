Medical SDEC Demand and Capacity Tool

A bottom-up model of Same Day Emergency Care demand and capacity at Lister. Set the current state from shift-level staffing and the hourly demand profile, then test a future state by adjusting demand and capacity assumptions. Every input is editable.

Method

How to Use the Model

STEP 1
Capacity Inputs
Enter each medical and nursing shift line — WTE, shift start and end, and the expected patients seen per person per hour. Seeded from the SDEC context note and monthly audit; edit any cell.
STEP 2
Demand Inputs
Enter the average patients present in SDEC each hour, 07:00–24:00, split by referral source. Switch between the weekday and weekend demand profiles.
STEP 3
Current State
Review demand against capacity, hour by hour, for medical and nursing. See where and when SDEC is over or under capacity today.
STEP 4
Future State
Toggle demand and capacity assumptions to test a future state, and read the change against today — including an indicative cost or saving.
Step 1

Capacity Inputs — Per Staff Group

Input shift-level data for each staff group: WTE, shift start and end times, and the expected patients seen per person per hour (medical). Each line also has its own Cost/WTE — defaults to the global assumption below but can be overridden per line item where actual grade/band costs are known. For nursing, tick the lines that carry patients in the bays (“Bay?”) — these add capacity at the patients-per-nurse ratio set in the assumptions; co-ordinator, triage, hello and in-reach roles are on the floor but do not add capacity. Seeded from the SDEC context note and monthly audit (GP 0.5, twilight IMT 0.6, registrar 0.67, consultant 1.0 patients/hr). Nursing shift times are placeholders — confirm with the roster lead.

Weekend view — switches the model to the weekend demand profile only (and the staff lines marked “Weekend”). Turn off to return to the weekday view.
Medical / Clinical (Update Inputs Here)

Patients assessed per clinician per hour is the whole-shift average throughput, including breaks and documentation. WTE follows the audit’s expected numbers (consultant ×1, registrar ×2, SHO ×3); the SHO rate is an assumption within the 0.5–1/hr envelope. Edit any cell.

Nursing (Update Inputs Here)

From the SDEC nursing template. “Bay?” ticked = the line carries patients and adds capacity; “Patients safely managed per nurse” is the number of patients each nurse on that line holds (overrides the global patients-per-nurse ratio in the assumptions; leave blank to use the global default). Unticked = fixed role (NIC co-ordinator, triage, hello, in-reach, EMT). Weekend template is lighter (bay ×2, EMT ×1). Shift times are placeholders.

Step 2

Demand Inputs — Patients Attending, 07:00–24:00

The demand profile is the average number of patients present in SDEC each hour, taken from the activity export (Apr–Jul 2026). Weekday demand peaks at ~21 patients present around 15:00; weekend runs about half that. Below the profile, the same demand is broken into its referral sources from the monthly audit — ED pull (~38%), follow-up (~27%), GP (~22%), hot clinics (~14%) and 111. Editing an hour changes the shape; editing a source rescales the day. Arrivals per hour are derived from the profile and the assumed length of stay.

Step 3

Current State — Demand vs Capacity

Today’s demand against today’s capacity, hour by hour, for the selected day type. The nursing view asks: are there enough bay nurses for the patients present? The medical view asks: can the clinicians on shift keep pace with arrivals, or does a queue build? Medical and nursing capacity are never added together — the overall service view classifies each hour by whichever, if either, is under pressure.

What This Means
    Demand vs Capacity, by Hour
    Pick a view below. Overall service shows a simple hour-by-hour status — within capacity, medical constraint, nursing constraint, both, or closed — because medical capacity (a flow measure) and nursing capacity (a concurrency measure) are never added together. Medical detail shows arrivals against seeing capacity and the queue that forms. Nursing detail shows patients present against nursing capacity (bay nurses × patients-per-nurse). Each detail chart has its own hour-by-hour balance bar underneath: blue/green means spare capacity, red/amber means demand is exceeding capacity that hour.
    Step 4

    Future State — Demand vs Capacity

    Test a change. On the demand side, dial each referral source up or down (or type an explicit attendances/day figure), apply an overall growth factor, or model better flow with a shorter length of stay. On the capacity side, switch on a ready-made preset lever, or pull in every current-state shift line and add to it directly. The charts below redraw for the future state, with the current state shown as a faint dashed line for comparison, and the headline change — including the net WTE and cost of every addition — quantified below.
